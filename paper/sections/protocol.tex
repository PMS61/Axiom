\section{Data and Protocol}
\subsection{Datasets}
% PROTOCOL: dataset name anki-revlogs-10k (not a result).
The Anki data is the public anki-revlogs-10k collection \cite{ankirevlogs10k}.
% PROTOCOL: EVALUATION.md 2 (user draw, card cap 1000). Not a result.
We draw 100 users at random and keep at most 1,000 cards per user, chosen at random.
Each card keeps its full history.
We repeat the cheap models on two more samples of 100 users, with seeds 1 and 2, and report the spread.
% FACTS: Anki robustness: seeds 0, 1, 2 (100 users each)
The Duolingo traces come from the study that introduced half-life regression \cite{settles2016trainable}.
We use all Duolingo users.

\subsection{Events}
One row of a revlog is one review.
We drop rows with rating 0, which are manual reschedules, and keep filtered-deck reviews, which carry real ratings and durations.
We drop cards whose first kept review is not a new card, because their history is truncated.
A review is recalled ($y=1$) if its rating is Hard, Good or Easy, and not recalled otherwise.
Gaps are measured over the kept rows only, so every model sees the same gap.

\subsection{Duolingo events}
Each Duolingo row is one practice session of one lexeme by one user.
A card is a (user, lexeme) pair, and rows are sorted by timestamp, then by lexeme id, then by file order.
% PROTOCOL: label threshold, EVALUATION.md 10.1. Not a result.
The recorded recall probability $p_{\mathrm{recall}}$ is thresholded: a row is recalled ($y=1$) when $p_{\mathrm{recall}} \ge 0.5$.
So $y$ is a threshold of a recorded probability, not an observed yes or no answer, and the metrics measure agreement with that threshold.
FSRS sees only two grades on this data, Good for a recalled row and Again otherwise.
A card's first row starts its replay and is never scored, because its earlier history is unknown.
The scored gaps reach at most 11 days, so only the 1d, 2-3d and 4-14d gap bins are populated.
% FACTS: Duolingo scored gaps: at most 11 days, so only the 1d, 2-3d and 4-14d bins are populated

\subsection{Split and one-step-ahead prediction}
% PROTOCOL: EVALUATION.md 4 (80th percentile cutoff). Not a result.
For each user we take the 80th percentile of the day offsets of that user's kept reviews as a cutoff.
Reviews before the cutoff are TRAIN, and later reviews are TEST.
All parameters are fitted on TRAIN rows only, and they are frozen for the test period.
A prediction for a TEST event may use every earlier review of the same card, including earlier TEST outcomes.
It may not use the event itself or any later event.

\subsection{Scored events}
A TEST event is scored when it is not a card's first review and its gap is at least one day.
Same-day reviews still enter the history of later predictions, but they are not scored.
This follows the inter-day convention of srs-benchmark \cite{srsbenchmark}.
On Anki there are 95,400 scored events.
% FACTS: Anki, Scored events: 95400
% PROTOCOL: scoring rule, EVALUATION.md 4. Not a result.

\subsection{Metrics}
Let $y_i \in \{0,1\}$ be the outcome and $p_i$ the predicted recall for scored event $i$, out of $N$ events.
% PROTOCOL: metric definitions and bin counts, EVALUATION.md 6. Not a result.
Log loss is $-\frac{1}{N}\sum_i [y_i \log p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)]$, with $p_i$ clipped to $[10^{-6}, 1-10^{-6}]$.
We also report the Brier score, AUC (the Mann--Whitney statistic), and the expected calibration error (ECE) over ten equal-width bins.
The mean bias is the mean prediction minus the mean outcome.
% PROTOCOL: gap bin labels 1d, 2-3d, 4-14d, 15-60d, 61d+ (EVALUATION.md 10.4). Not a result.
Scored events are grouped by gap into 1d, 2-3d, 4-14d, 15-60d and 61d+ bins.
Log loss is the main metric, and every other metric is reported with it.

\subsection{Paired differences and user-clustered bootstrap}
For a model $m$ and reference $r$, the paired difference is $\mathrm{metric}(m) - \mathrm{metric}(r)$.
% PROTOCOL: 1000 bootstrap resamples, seed 0 (EVALUATION.md 6). Not a result.
We resample users with replacement, 1,000 times with seed 0.
Each resample keeps all events of the drawn users, and both models use the same users.
% PROTOCOL: EVALUATION.md 6 (1000 resamples, seed 0, 95% CI). Not a result.
The 95\% interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap differences.
We resample users, not events, because reviews by one learner are correlated.
Resampling events would give intervals that are too narrow.

\subsection{Floors}
We fit three constant floors on TRAIN rows only, using rows that are not a card's first review and have a gap of at least one day.
The constant (global) is the mean recall rate over all such rows.
The constant (per user) is each user's own mean, and a user with no such rows gets the global rate.
The constant (per user, smoothed) shrinks each user's rate towards the global rate with a fixed pseudo-count $m$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat p_u = \frac{k_u + m\,\bar p}{n_u + m},
\end{equation}
where $k_u$ and $n_u$ are the user's recalled and total TRAIN rows, and $\bar p$ is the global rate.
We set $m = 10$ before any run and did not tune it.
% FACTS: Protocol, data and disclosures: m = 10 (source: docs/EVALUATION.md section 6)
The floors use no test labels.

\subsection{Same-session leakage}
Duolingo records every review in a practice session with one timestamp.
Our first GBDT computed user statistics from earlier rows in the file, and that included other rows with the same timestamp.
A review could therefore see outcomes from its own session, which is leakage in the sense of \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.
On the Duolingo development sample, the frozen GBDT's validation log loss was 0.2473 with this leak and 0.2578 after the fix.
% FACTS: Duolingo validation, frozen GBDT, log loss with the same-session leak: 0.2473. Duolingo validation, frozen GBDT, log loss after the fix: 0.2578
The fix groups rows by timestamp, so a row only uses reviews with strictly earlier timestamps.
Anki reviews are timed one by one and are already in order, so Anki uses per-row updates.
The same grouping applies to rows of one card that share a timestamp.
Section 7 lists this leak as a disclosure.
