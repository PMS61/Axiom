\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Memory models for recall.}
Half-life regression \cite{settles2016trainable} models recall as an exponential forgetting curve whose half-life depends on the counts of past correct and wrong answers.
FSRS \cite{ye2022stochastic, srsbenchmark} schedules reviews with a memory model that tracks stability and difficulty.
We use its sixth version as a baseline.
ACT-R models of spacing \cite{pavlik2005practice} explain the spacing effect through activation of memory traces.
DASH and DAS3H \cite{lindsey2014improving, choffin2019das3h} add learner and item effects to a model of the practice history.
KARL \cite{shu2024karl} uses the history of related cards to predict recall.
Our v2 and v3 models follow the tradition of per-user effects.

\paragraph{Public benchmarks.}
The srs-benchmark \cite{srsbenchmark} compares FSRS, HLR, DASH, sequence models (GRU, LSTM and RWKV) and simple per-user floors on a large public collection of Anki reviews.
Its data is the source of our Anki dataset \cite{ankirevlogs10k}.
Its moving-average floor, which uses no memory model, beats FSRS-6 on that benchmark \cite{srsbenchmark}.
Its RWKV model reads a user's full review history across cards \cite{srsbenchmark}.
This is close to the cross-card user signal in our GBDT, so that signal is not new.

\paragraph{Boosting in related tasks.}
The SLAM shared task on second-language acquisition modelling \cite{settles2018slam} drew many systems.
Osika et al. describe an ensemble approach to the same task \cite{osika2018slam}.
We use LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} for our GBDT.

\paragraph{Leakage.}
Leakage, where information from the target or from the future enters a model's features or training data, is a known cause of over-optimistic benchmark results \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.
The same-session pitfall in Section 3 is an instance of this general problem.

\paragraph{What is new here.}
We did not find a tree-based recall model with FSRS-state features in the spaced-repetition work we reviewed, so we present GBDT as a new comparison point.
We do not claim priority for using other cards' history.
Our contributions are the floor-based comparison, the leakage audit, and the Bayesian versus tree-based comparison on two datasets.
