# Paper outline (Phase 3 input; orchestrator, 2026-11-15)

Working title: **"Beating the Floor: A Leakage-Audited Benchmark of Recall Prediction for Spaced Repetition"**.
Target: EDM 2027. A full paper is 10 pages plus references (check the official call). The submission is anonymous.
Every number comes from `paper/FACTS.md`; if a number is not there, it does not go in the paper.

## 1. Introduction (about 1 page)
- Spaced-repetition schedulers rely on a recall predictor. Models are usually compared with each other, and rarely
  with a floor.
- The claims:
  - (i) on Duolingo, a smoothed per-user constant beats FSRS-6, HLR and our MAP Bayesian models;
  - (ii) a GBDT using FSRS-state features and the user's own running history beats FSRS-6 on both datasets by every metric;
  - (iii) an MCMC Bayesian model is the second-best model on Duolingo but barely beats the floor;
  - (iv) a same-session leakage pitfall in the Duolingo traces.
- Contributions: a bullet list matching these claims, plus open code.

## 2. Related work (about 0.75 page)
- Memory models: HLR (Settles & Meeder 2016), FSRS, ACT-R, DASH and DAS3H (Lindsey et al. 2014; Choffin et al. EDM 2019), KAR3L.
- The srs-benchmark: GRU, LSTM and RWKV; its AVG and MOVING-AVG floors. Note that MOVING-AVG beats FSRS-6 there.
- GBDTs in neighbouring tasks: SLAM 2018 (Osika et al.), knowledge tracing.
- Benchmark hygiene and leakage in educational data mining.
- Sources: EVALUATION.md 12.2 and the black book references.

## 3. Data and protocol (about 1.25 pages)
- Anki: anki-revlogs-10k, 100 users, plus the seeds-1/2 robustness samples. Duolingo: Settles & Meeder traces, all 115,222 users.
- Event definition, the per-user 80th-percentile time cutoff, one-step-ahead prediction, and the scored events.
- Metrics: log loss, Brier, AUC, ECE and mean bias, with a user-clustered paired bootstrap.
- Floors: global constant, per-user constant and smoothed per-user constant (m = 10, fixed in advance).
- Leakage audit: a figure or box on the same-session timestamps and how they are grouped.

## 4. Models (about 1.25 pages)
- FSRS-6 (default and optimised), HLR, LagLogit.
- v1: Bayesian dynamic retention model, Gibbs sampling with Polya-Gamma augmentation (with the GPU port).
- v2 and v3: hierarchical FSRS-form models fitted by MAP.
- GBDT: feature groups, frozen settings, and the validation-only selection.

## 5. Results (about 2.5 pages)
- Table 1: overall metrics on both datasets, with the floors at the top.
- Table 2: paired differences vs FSRS-opt and vs the smoothed constant, with user-win shares.
- Figure: log loss by gap bin (from `paper/data/*_gaps.csv`).
- Figure: reliability diagrams.
- Robustness: the Anki seeds; the GBDT overfitting checks (EVALUATION 12.1: validation vs test, unseen users).
- srs-benchmark (Phase 2): GBDT vs FSRS-6, RWKV, GRU, AVG and MOVING-AVG on 1,000 held-out users, with the
  unweighted and size-weighted means.

## 6. Discussion (about 1 page)
- Why constants are strong on Duolingo: high base rate (0.92), short gaps and lexeme effects.
- The ranking flips between datasets.
- The Bayesian vs ML trade-off: interpretability and uncertainty vs accuracy.
- GBDT has no memory state, so it is a predictor and not a scheduler. A hybrid is future work.

## 7. Limitations and disclosures (about 0.5 page)
- Every item in EVALUATION.md 11.4 (a)-(i).
- One split; the Anki sample size; v1's λ does not converge; the MAP fits; FSRS-opt falls back to defaults on most Duolingo users;
  there is no scheduling simulation.
